DAY 4 RESEARCH NOTES — ESG Greenwashing Detector
Sector: Airlines
Companies researched today: Delta Air Lines, Lufthansa Group

============================================================
DELTA AIR LINES
============================================================

EMISSIONS DATA (2024, reported)
- Total emissions: ~80.8 billion kg CO2e
- Scope 1: ~37.3 billion kg CO2e (primarily mobile combustion / jet fuel)
- Scope 2: ~165.3 million kg CO2e (purchased electricity)
- Scope 3: ~23.1 billion kg CO2e (largest contributor: fuel and energy-related activities, followed by investments and employee commute)

For comparison, 2023 figures:
- Total: ~56.0 billion kg CO2e
- Scope 1: ~35.9 billion kg CO2e
- Scope 2: ~188.4 million kg CO2e
- Scope 3: ~20.1 billion kg CO2e

CLAIM 1 — Specific (has real substance)
Text: "Reduce well-to-wake Scope 1 and Scope 3 jet fuel GHG emissions 45% per revenue ton kilometer by 2035, from a 2019 baseline."
Type: Future target
Signals present: percentage (45%), defined scope (1 and 3), baseline year (2019), target year (2035)
Source: Delta Difference 2024 Report / SBTi-approved target
Notes: This is the "gold standard" specific claim — everything you need to fact-check later against actual 2035 performance.

CLAIM 2 — Vague / weakened over time (strong greenwashing signal)
Text: Originally called net-zero by 2050 the company's "ultimate goal"; recently reframed on Delta's own sustainability webpage as an "aspiration." Also quietly removed public reference to a 2030 sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) goal, while a company spokesperson told press Delta "remains committed" to 10% SAF by 2030.
Type: General commitment (weakened) / inconsistent public messaging
Signals present: no new number provided for the softened language; contradiction between public page and spokesperson statement
Source: ESG Dive, April 2026
Notes: Excellent example claim for your project — same company, two different public commitments to the same goal within a short time window, one weaker than the other.

CLAIM 3 — Vague, contradicts own reported data
Text: A Delta sustainability executive stated "anything beyond Scope 1 is like a rounding error."
Type: Performance/framing claim
Signals present: no number given; directly contradicted by Delta's own reported Scope 3 figure (23.1B kg — actually LARGER than Scope 1's 37.3B is close but Scope 3 is still material, not a rounding error)
Source: Trellis, Dec 2024
Notes: Good example of a claim that doesn't match the company's own disclosed numbers.

External assurance: Delta references third-party verification of its net-zero progress reporting in its Delta Difference Report (not fully detailed in search results — verify page number when you read the actual PDF).

============================================================
LUFTHANSA GROUP
============================================================

EMISSIONS DATA
- Lufthansa's 2024 Sustainability Fact Sheet reports Scope 2 and 3 emissions as "audited with limited assurance" (see report for exact tonnage — not fully captured in search results, confirm on PDF read).
- 2024 was the first year Lufthansa prepared its non-financial declaration under CSRD / ESRS standards (EU's new mandatory reporting framework).
- SAF-related savings example: of a reported reduction amount, 63,943 tons were attributed to direct SAF combustion savings (Scope 1) and 8,009 tons to upstream supply chain savings (Scope 3).

CLAIM 1 — Regulatory action (real, documented — strongest case study)
Text: A German court ordered Lufthansa to stop advertising flights using sustainability claims that relied on inaccurate calculations and carbon offsetting.
Type: Performance / marketing claim, ruled misleading
Source: Lexology Pro, March 2025
Notes: This is a real legal precedent — use this to justify your project's premise in your README/methodology page.

CLAIM 2 — Regulatory action (UK)
Text: UK's Advertising Standards Authority banned a Lufthansa ad claiming the airline was "protecting the planet."
Type: General commitment (marketing claim)
Source: Euronews, March 2023
Notes: No number, no baseline, no target — exactly the "vague claim" pattern from Day 3, and regulators agreed it was misleading enough to ban.

CLAIM 3 — Ongoing EU investigation (sector-wide, includes Lufthansa subsidiaries)
Text: The European Commission is questioning 20 airlines — including Lufthansa Group's Brussels Airlines, Lufthansa, SWISS, Austrian Airlines, Air Dolomiti, and Eurowings — over whether CO2 claims (offsetting, SAF) are backed by sound scientific evidence. Triggered by a complaint from BEUC (European Consumer Organisation) naming 17 airlines.
Source: fl360aero, 2024 / Reuters
Notes: Confirms Lufthansa's greenwashing exposure isn't a one-off — it's an active, multi-brand regulatory concern.

CLAIM 4 — Industry pledge (response to scrutiny)
Text: Lufthansa and KLM are among 21 European carriers that pledged to stop making misleading environmental claims, including ending "green fare" marketing language that implies a ticket purchase directly cancels out emissions.
Source: Aviation A2Z, Nov 2025
Notes: Useful as a "did they follow through" check later — you could track whether marketing language actually changed after this pledge.

SECTOR-WIDE CONTEXT (useful for your methodology page)
============================================================
A study of 16 major European airline groups found:
- Scope 1 CO2 reported by 94% of airlines
- Scope 2 reported by only 56%
- Scope 3 reported by only 50%
- Non-CO2 emissions disclosed by just 38%

This confirms Scope 3 under-disclosure is a real, sector-wide pattern — not something unique to the companies you picked. Good sentence for your project's methodology/limitations page.
